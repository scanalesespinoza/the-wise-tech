# The architecture we inhabit
**Source:** https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/la-arquitectura-que-se-habita-sergio-canales-espinoza-igiqe  
**Author:** Sergio Canales Espinoza  
**Date:** 2025-07-29  
**Extraction:** structured-summary (faithful representation, no full copy)

## Central idea
The author argues that every architecture—physical or digital—must be designed for the people who inhabit it. He urges teams to prioritize experience and reliability over technology trends, reminding us that decisions affect those who depend on the system. Inhabiting what we build requires observation, listening, and continuous adjustment.

## Key points (bullet list)
- Architecture is validated by the usefulness and simplicity perceived by the product’s users.
- Trends matter only when they reinforce purpose and reliability; they should not dictate decisions.
- Experiencing one’s own software reveals real priorities and removes ornamentation.
- Frequent connection with users and teams keeps the human focus alive.
- Inhabited architecture means designing trustworthy, respectful digital spaces.

## Argument structure
1. Contrasts an instrumental view of software with the idea of inhabited spaces.
2. Explores the value of intention behind each technological decision.
3. Closes with a call to build reliable systems that inspire trust.

## Relevant data/examples
- Personal accounts of using one’s own development to prioritize clarity and quick response.
- Recommendations to keep active listening between builders and end users.

## Limitations/scope
- Conceptual text without metrics; needs operational guidance alongside.
- Applies best when teams can engage in direct feedback loops; less useful in highly siloed environments.

## Practical conclusion (≤120 words)
Designing habitable systems demands a discipline of constant observation. Teams should experience their own product, gather immediate feedback, and judge every technological decision by the usefulness it delivers to people. Choosing simplicity, ensuring reliability, and making contributions visible strengthen trust and adoption.
